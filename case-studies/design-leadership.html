Research Report

Emerging Patterns: Design Leadership in 2026

A public research report on the state of design leadership, produced by the Spring 2026 MDes Design Leadership seminar at the Sam Fox School of Design & Visual Arts, Washington University in St. Louis, led by Doug Powell.

Spring 2026 research
Summer 2026 writing & publication

Led qualitative research and analysis; co-developed the interview service blueprint; co-wrote the report

Jillian Jackson, Tejas Garg, Matt Grossman, Shawn Hao, Brian Lee, Jude Licea, Justin Ling, Andrew Pandji, Bradley Spudich, Ashley Xiang, with Doug Powell

MDes Design Leadership Seminar, Sam Fox School, WashU

Overview

The question

How do designers actually become leaders, and what lets them keep leading as the profession keeps changing?

The research

  • 119 design leaders surveyed across 31 topics
  • 11 in-depth interviews with leaders across a global range of organizations
  • Published as an interactive report with 5 themes and a filterable data-explore view

Research

A 31-topic survey, completed by 119 design leaders.

Qual

  • 11 recorded interviews, chosen for range across organizations, geographies, and career stages
  • Surfaced the “why” behind the stats, and language that became organizing ideas for the report (e.g., “portfolio career,” the missing “ladder”)

Service Design Blueprint for the Interview Process

The problem

11 interviews, 10 team members. How do you keep the process consistent without flattening the depth that makes interviews valuable?

The approach

A service blueprint mapping both sides of the process:

  • Front-stage (interviewee): invitation → scheduling → pre-interview framing → interview → follow-up
  • Back-stage (team): discussion guide development → interviewer prep → recording/transcription → handoff to synthesis

Insight Synthesis

Method

Affinity mapping and thematic analysis, looking for where quantitative insights aligned with or contradicted qualitative remarks.

Summer Writing Process

Research wrapped by the end of the semester, but writing and publishing happened over the summer in collaboration with Doug Powell and Andrew Pandji.

Key decisions over the summer

  • Narrowing to 5 themes
  • Selecting quotes that best represent our thesis
  • Building the interactive data view, combining the quantitative data visualization with the narrative style of the interview quotes
  • Editorial critique with Doug Powell, which brought the narrative and tone together to represent our research appropriately
